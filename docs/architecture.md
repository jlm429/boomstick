# Architecture

Boomstick is a browser-first, single-player arena combat prototype.

| Area                                                | Ownership                                |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Application screens, menu, HUD, pause dialog        | React                                    |
| Authoritative application phase and run state       | Zustand                                  |
| Scene graph, camera, lighting, visual animation     | React Three Fiber / Three.js             |
| Physics world, arena, target, player, zombie bodies | Rapier                                   |
| Movement, encounter, and combat rules               | Rendering-independent TypeScript utility |

`App` switches between React screens using one phase machine: `main-menu`, `about`, `controls`, `arena-entry`, `playing`, `paused`, `dying`, `defeated`, and `training-complete`. The store records whether the active control mode is `desktop` or `touch`. In desktop mode, the native Canvas is passed directly to `App`; Enter Arena, Resume, Restart, and Retry request pointer lock from their click handlers. The browser's `pointerlockchange` event alone confirms desktop play, and lock loss pauses the run. On browsers that report touch points and a coarse primary pointer, those actions begin an explicit touch session without claiming pointer lock. Store transitions preserve the invariant that `playing` has either Canvas-owned desktop pointer lock or an active touch session, never neither.

`GameViewport` keeps arena and combat presentation outside Rapier's asynchronous boundary. The rendering-independent layouts in `src/game/arena.ts` and `src/game/targets.ts` supply arena geometry, repeated light-fixture placement, and fixed targets; arena blocks always have matching Rapier colliders, while target colliders exist only through the training countdown and light fixtures use forgiving rendered hit volumes without physics colliders. `CombatScene` owns transient weapon, broken-light, and training-target presentation state. Each `TargetVisual` owns its health, while `CombatScene` mirrors target health to drive the training countdown through `src/game/encounter.ts`. It delegates ammunition and reload rules to `src/game/shooting.ts` and reports weapon and encounter presentation state to the React HUD. Player health and the `alive`, `dying`, and `dead` flow stay authoritative in `src/game/store.ts`, with thresholds and death timing in `src/game/playerHealth.ts`. `RunScene` owns the encounter state that controls physics composition, including target colliders and the training zombie encounter, and records distinct corpse removals through completion. `TrainingZombieEncounter` creates the five keyed zombies from `TRAINING_ZOMBIE_SPAWNS` and owns their shared attack-slot coordinator. Each keyed `Zombie` owns its presence, while its actor owns health, behavior, lifetime motion variation, animation mixer, attack controller, effects, audio, and Rapier body. Reusable zombie attack configuration and its timer-driven windup, hit, and cooldown cycle live in `src/game/zombieAttack.ts`; stable exclusive attack and overflow positions live in `src/game/zombieAttackSlots.ts`. Completion advances only after all five corpses have cleared, which pauses the scene, ends active controls, releases pointer lock when present, hides the HUD, and shows the React completion overlay.

`CombatScene` accepts fire and reload input only during active play and raycasts each fired pellet against rendered scene geometry so the nearest collision controls hit feedback. Desktop pointer and keyboard listeners retain their Canvas-owned lock checks. The touch overlay owns pointer classification according to the gesture contract in `docs/controls.md` and writes movement, look, jump, aim-fire, fire, and reload into the explicit rendering-independent state in `src/game/input.ts`. `Player` consumes aim-fire by applying the tapped world direction to its authoritative yaw, pitch, and camera before forwarding one ordinary fire action to `CombatScene`. Drag look and tap aim therefore share the same camera authority, pitch limits, weapon presentation, and camera-forward hitscan. A live target hit uses the raycast hit point in target-local space and its distance from the shot origin to calculate damage through the pure rules in `src/game/targets.ts`; depleted targets remain visible but stop participating in shot raycasts. Surface, live-target, and live-zombie hits select wall impact audio, while a light hit within the inclusive 18-unit destruction limit extinguishes that fixture and selects its one-time destruction audio. Each zombie uses a dynamic Rapier body with a capsule collider while alive, reads the player camera position, probes fixed collider geometry for direct or alternate steering, and switches explicitly among idle, chase, attack, hit, and dead behavior. Death removes its collider and fixes its body until the corpse expires and the keyed zombie unmounts. Each zombie samples one lifetime-stable speed multiplier shared by translation and run playback, then applies a one-time full-cycle phase offset when its run animation first starts. Pursuing zombies retain exclusive player-relative attack slots selected by nearest availability; overflow zombies wait at deterministic outer positions until a slot is released. Arrival and exit tolerances preserve slot ownership while suppressing small-motion steering jitter. Zombie hits spawn short-lived impact-point blood particles. The hit-reaction action runs at its centralized accelerated time scale, and behavior recovery uses its effective duration. A valid bite applies one configured hit after windup and starts cooldown, and its impact retriggers an entity-owned particle buffer near the camera. A zombie releases its slot when pursuit stops, play or player life ends, it dies, or it unmounts. Player death stops input and weapon actions, lowers and rolls the camera without moving the rigid body, fades to black, and presents Retry and Return to Main Menu actions. Killing a zombie stops its movement, collision, and bite audio immediately, then removes its corpse and owned runtime resources after the death animation and hold. Restart and Retry reset store-owned health and life state while the new `runId` remounts `RunScene` to reset the weapon, targets, lights, countdown, all five zombies, physics bodies, animation state, particles, attack timers, attack slots, camera offsets, and audio. Returning to the main menu unmounts the Canvas and performs the same cleanup. A Rapier startup delay therefore cannot remove the sky, lighting, visible level, targets, or weapon. The fixed-step physics world is paused outside play. `Player` installs keyboard and mouse listeners once per mount, while the touch overlay owns its pointer captures. Both input paths clear transient state on pause, blur, visibility loss, death, reset, and unmount. The render loop guards transforms, resets escaped bodies, clamps unsafe frame transitions, and follows the rigid body at eye height.

This boundary keeps gameplay additions testable and prevents UI work from coupling to physics presentation.
